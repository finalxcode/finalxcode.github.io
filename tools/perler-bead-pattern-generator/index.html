How to make a Perler bead pattern from a photo

  1. Pick a simple image. Bold shapes and clear outlines convert best. Crop tightly around the subject first.
  2. Set the width in beads. 29 beads fits one large pegboard; 52–58 beads is two boards wide and gives good detail for faces and characters.
  3. Limit the colors. 8–16 colors is usually enough. Fewer colors means fewer bead bags to buy and a cleaner result.
  4. Check the stray-color cleanup. It merges single, isolated beads into the surrounding color so you are not placing one odd bead in a sea of red.
  5. Download or print. The PNG has a code in every cell and board lines in blue; the CSV is your shopping list with 10% extra for spills.

Tips for better bead patterns

  • For pixel art and game sprites, tick pixel art and set the width to the sprite's real pixel width so each pixel becomes exactly one bead.
  • Logos on a white background: tick treat white background as empty so you only place beads where the design is.
  • Dithering helps photos with soft gradients, but makes the pattern harder to build. Leave it off for cartoons.
  • Screen colors differ from real beads. Compare with a physical color chart before you buy.

FAQ

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser; the image never leaves your device.

Does it work for Hama, Artkal and mini beads?

Yes. Pick 5 mm for Perler, Hama midi and Artkal S, or 2.6 mm for minis, and paste your brand's color chart to use its codes.

How many beads fit on one pegboard?

A large square pegboard is 29 × 29 pegs (841 beads). The generator draws a line at every board edge and counts the boards.

Are the color codes official Perler codes?

No. The default palette is generic. Paste an official chart into the custom palette box to get brand codes.

Can I sell items made from these patterns?

Patterns made from your own artwork are yours. Characters, logos and other people's art may be protected by copyright or trademark, so check before selling.
